## Session — the phased ladder, and what it caught (RB-21)

Built the calibration ladder the phased store-removal protocol had been missing
since 2026-08-02, when three docs started saying openly that the protocol was
*argued* to discriminate rather than *demonstrated* to.

The ladder does demonstrate it, and it also found the thing worth knowing. A SUT
that persists its raw store to the survive-dir violates the protocol's SUT
contract, and scores `norm_gain = 1.000` on the phased arm — identical, to four
decimal places, to a SUT that genuinely consolidates. The contract already warned
about this in prose. What was not established is that the protocol is *blind* to
its own violation: nothing in the phased number, its CI, or its post-reset window
distinguishes the two. Running the uniform arm alongside does distinguish them,
so the ladder now reports both arms in one table and the docs say to read the
pair.

The second finding is smaller and sharper. The partial rung landed on exactly
0.500 at migration fraction 0.5, which looked like a beautiful result and is
mostly an artifact: the task assigns object `i` attribute `i % A`, and an
every-other-episode selector migrates exactly the objects whose rules it also
migrates, so 2-hop transfer tracks 1-hop recall instead of compounding. An
alignment-free selector on the same fraction scores 0.344. Both rungs are now in
the ladder specifically so the number cannot be read as "half the capability
migrated".

Open question 2 in the notebook index — *a phased reference ladder does not
exist* — is closed. What replaces it is narrower and more useful: the phased arm
is not self-sufficient, and any consolidation claim made from it needs its
uniform companion to rule out the store-present explanation.

No spending; keyless and offline throughout. The whole ladder runs in about ten
seconds.
